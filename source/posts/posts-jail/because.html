Everyone has their own reason for making a website. I myself got bored of having all of my information on several different services, so I said: Why not try to pick coding back up and have all of my things in the same place?

If you go on anyone's website in the Indie Web, they'll probably also tell you why they made their website, and there's all kinds of reasons. But there's one reason in particular I want to talk about: social media.

9 times out of 10, I'll see someone list a reason that's along the lines of "I made my website because I hate social media," which is a perfectly fine reason, if not for the fact everyone I've seen that says this still uses a form of social media.

You despise it, sure, and yet you still use Discord, or Mastodon, or one of the many other "niche" not-social-media websites out there.Everyone does. It's really nothing to be ashamed of.

I won't lie to you and say that places like Twitter, TikTok, and Instagram aren't predatory—and that they don't encourage people to tear each other apart just so they can see some numbers go up—But I also won't lie to you and say that social media isn't a great way to find and connect with other people.

That's why I don't enjoy no-nuance "social media bad" manifestos. Why do you focus on all the negative parts of it, when that's the same thing these big names want you to do? To focus on only the bad parts of everything?

You don't need to have some enlightened reason to make a website. Did you make your website because you wanted to learn HTML one day? Great. Did you want to make a better-looking website than someone else's? Sure; you can just say that! Nobody is stopping you but yourself.
